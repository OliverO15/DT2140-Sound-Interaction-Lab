    • Interaction 3: Bubble level

    Use case: a spirit level, like the one in the iPhone Measure app, that you can use without looking at the screen, for example when hanging a shelf above your head. Place the phone on its long side (or flat) on a surface. When it is level, bubbles drip slowly and calmly. The further it tilts away from level, the faster the bubbles come, up to a rapid stream at 30° or more.

    Why: a traditional spirit level uses an air bubble in liquid, so bubble sounds are a natural metaphor for it. A slow, calm rhythm means "you're there", and a fast rhythm signals that something is off, much like a parking sensor beeping faster as you get closer. Changing the speed gives continuous feedback, so you can hear which way to adjust instead of just whether it is level or not.
