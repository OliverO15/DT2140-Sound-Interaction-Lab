    • Interaction 1: Boat throttle and steering

    Use case: a boat or jet-ski game, or a remote control for a toy boat, where you steer without looking at the screen. The phone works like a boat's throttle lever. Tilting it forward and back (rotation X) sets the engine: held upright at 90° the engine is stopped, held flat at 0° it runs at cruising speed (80%), and tilted past flat towards you it goes to full throttle. Engine speed and volume rise together, so you hear how hard the boat is pushing. Tilting the phone left or right (rotation Y) pans the engine sound to that side, like the boat turning.

    Why: pushing a lever forward to go faster is a familiar mapping from real boats, and an engine that revs up and gets louder is direct feedback that matches the gesture. Hearing the sound move left or right gives a sense of direction while turning, without needing to look at the screen.
